# Does the pigauto GNN add predictive value?

## Finding

In the two prespecified confirmatory scenarios, pigauto's neural contribution reduced observation-level held-out RMSE relative to the same fitted model with only its neural contribution removed. Each comparison used 500 independent datasets, and both passed the campaign's Holm-adjusted test, simultaneous Bonferroni interval, and 2% practical-effect rule.

The result applies to the current `impute()` default (`phylo_signal_gate = FALSE`) in these repeated-observation covariate simulations. It does not establish that the full pigauto model beats non-neural alternatives in every scenario. In particular, the separately fitted phylogenetic GLS had substantially lower RMSE than pigauto in the interactive scenario.

## Design and target

The frozen contract is in [ADEMP.md](ADEMP.md), with the five-scenario allocation in [scenario_manifest.csv](../../../script/gnn_incremental/scenario_manifest.csv). Each replicate generated a 500-tip birth-death tree and an independent Brownian species effect with variance multiplier 0.3 or 0.05. Species had a truncated Poisson number of repeated observations (mean parameter 5, range 1 to 15). Each observation had ten independent standard-normal covariates and independent Gaussian observation error with standard deviation 0.5. The covariate function was nonlinear, interactive, linear, or null according to the frozen manifest. The variance multiplier is a Brownian variance component; it is not Pagel's lambda.

Exactly half the species were withheld completely. Among the remaining species, a target of 20% of observations was masked while retaining at least one observed row per species. The scored outcomes were not passed to model fitting. Results are reported for all outer holdouts, fully unobserved species, and partly observed species.

The primary estimand is the dataset-level paired MSE difference, full pigauto minus its same-fit neural-off counterfactual. The ablation transfers the neural gate weight to the Brownian weight and preserves the fitted mean weight, model state, and linear covariate effect. It therefore isolates the fitted neural contribution within that model. A separate phylogenetic random-intercept GLS used the same observed rows, tree, and predeclared covariate basis (ten linear terms, ten squares, and 45 pairwise interactions). Mean, linear OLS, and polynomial OLS were additional reference methods. These comparators are not claimed to form a general performance ceiling.

The public `impute()` default `phylo_signal_gate = FALSE` was the confirmatory policy. Both policy arms used `lambda_mode = "estimate"`, the current default. The `phylo_signal_gate = TRUE` arm is a policy sensitivity only. The lower-level `fit_pigauto()` direct-call default differs; it was not the entry point in this campaign.

## Default-policy results

Pooled RMSE below is the square root of the mean dataset-level MSE, so each independent dataset contributes equally. Relative RMSE reduction is `1 - RMSE(full) / RMSE(neural-off)`. Its interval is a bootstrap over independent datasets. The two confirmatory MSE-difference intervals are simultaneous family-wise 95% Bonferroni intervals; their paired one-sided tests were adjusted with Holm's method. The tabulated GLS, OLS, polynomial OLS, and mean values are descriptive reference results.

| Scenario | Pigauto full / neural-off RMSE | Relative reduction (95% dataset-bootstrap interval) | GLS RMSE | Linear OLS RMSE | Polynomial OLS RMSE | Mean RMSE |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Nonlinear, variance multiplier 0.3 (confirmatory) | 0.764 / 0.924 | 17.3% (17.2%, 17.5%) | 0.768 | 0.877 | 0.878 | 1.228 |
| Interactive, variance multiplier 0.3 (confirmatory) | 0.911 / 1.213 | 24.9% (24.8%, 25.0%) | 0.602 | 1.236 | 0.742 | 1.226 |
| Nonlinear, variance multiplier 0.05 (control) | 0.709 / 0.882 | 19.7% (19.5%, 19.8%) | 0.720 | 0.745 | 0.734 | 1.137 |
| Linear recovery (control) | 0.654 / 0.787 | 16.8% (16.7%, 17.0%) | 0.603 | 0.710 | 0.740 | 1.224 |
| No covariate signal (control) | 0.593 / 0.591 | -0.2% (-0.3%, -0.2%) | 0.603 | 0.715 | 0.744 | 0.710 |

For the nonlinear confirmatory scenario, the mean paired MSE difference was -0.2705 (MCSE 0.00148), with simultaneous interval [-0.2738, -0.2672] and Holm-adjusted p < 0.001. The relative RMSE reduction was 17.3% (bootstrap interval 17.19% to 17.48%). For the interactive confirmatory scenario, the mean paired MSE difference was -0.6420 (MCSE 0.00167), with simultaneous interval [-0.6457, -0.6382] and Holm-adjusted p < 0.001. The relative reduction was 24.9% (24.77% to 25.04%). Both met the prespecified criteria and are classified **supported** for these scenarios.

The low-signal and linear-recovery controls also had positive within-fit reductions, but they were not confirmatory tests. The no-signal control had a small increase in error with the neural contribution: the estimated relative reduction was -0.2%. This provides a concrete limit on the claim: a useful branch in covariate-signal settings does not imply benefit when the covariate function is absent.

The non-neural references materially qualify the result. In the nonlinear scenario, pooled RMSE was 0.764 for pigauto, 0.767 for GLS, and 0.876 for polynomial OLS. This overall near parity hides a stratum difference: for partly observed species, pigauto RMSE was 0.801 versus 0.749 for GLS, while for fully withheld species the values were 0.756 and 0.771. These are descriptive comparisons, but they show why the aggregate result should not be read as uniform performance across missingness patterns. In the interactive scenario, GLS RMSE was 0.602 compared with 0.911 for pigauto, and polynomial OLS RMSE was 0.742. The interactive generator is exactly represented by the predeclared polynomial interaction basis, so these comparators can recover its covariate function directly. The analysis supports an incremental contribution within pigauto's fit in that scenario. It does not show that pigauto's full model is the strongest predictor among these references.

## Missingness strata and gate sensitivity

Under the default policy, relative reductions for completely withheld versus partly observed species were 15.1% and 25.4% in the nonlinear scenario, and 23.7% and 29.7% in the interactive scenario. Each stratum had 500 paired datasets. These stratified estimates are descriptive and did not receive separate confirmatory tests.

In the `phylo_signal_gate = TRUE` sensitivity arm, relative reductions were 15.7% in the nonlinear scenario, 21.4% in the interactive scenario, 1.9% in the low-signal control, 14.9% in linear recovery, and -0.2% in the null control. The gate triggered in 8.6%, 12.2%, 89.6%, 9.6%, and 0% of those scenarios, respectively. As designed, this arm frequently routed the low-signal control away from the neural corner. It is a sensitivity analysis and does not change the confirmatory classification under the shipped default.

## Interpretation and limits

The strongest supported statement is that, for the two tested repeated-observation covariate signals and the current public `impute()` default, the fitted GNN correction improved held-out predictions over the same fit with that correction removed. The gain was also present in the low-signal and linear controls, while the null control showed a small loss. Separately fitted GLS and polynomial OLS results show that the gain does not establish broad method superiority.

This study used one tree-generating process, 500 species, ten standard-normal covariates, Gaussian observation error, and one structured missingness design. It did not test the other seven trait types, single-observation workflows, imputation uncertainty, conformal coverage, downstream inference, or external datasets. Prediction intervals and inferential calibration are outside this campaign. The results cannot be generalized to every empirical dataset or cited as a distribution-free guarantee.

The campaign also corrected an implementation bug in the optional signal-gate path: the signal estimate now masks internal validation and test cells before calculation. The new regression test has a direct `phytools::phylosig()` oracle and deliberately extreme held-out values. This source fix changes no public argument or default. The `TRUE` policy results apply to this corrected branch and should not be retroactively assigned to older releases.

Frequentist and BACE multiple imputation under Rubin's rules

What the campaign shows

Design

Each simulated dataset is a random coalescent tree with 7 traits (continuous c1, c2; count; proportion; binary; ordinal; three-class categorical) plus a fully observed driver, generated as vec(L) ~ N(0, Σρ ⊗ Vλ) with 30% of cells missing completely at random. Every arm imputes the same datasets (same seed), M = 20 times, and every estimate is pooled with Rubin's rules. In this design the true PGLS slope of c2 on c1 and the true phylogenetic correlation both equal ρ.

Discrete traits (n = 100 and 300)

The same datasets (all 12 cells at n = 100 and 300, 200 seeds each) were imputed again, keeping all 20 imputations, and scored on the binary, ordinal and three-class traits. The BACE arms are the same fits as above, re-run. The frequentist arms use castor's Mk model one trait at a time: freq A redraws the transition rates by parametric bootstrap before each imputation, freq B keeps the fitted rates, and both use flexible rates (all rates different for the binary and categorical traits, stepwise with every rate different for the ordinal one). Simulation v1's equal-rates model runs as a comparison arm. Each set of missing classes is drawn jointly on the tree.

    Failures and what was fixed

      What this means for the BACE paper

        What this does not cover

        • One simulation design. Brownian motion with Pagel's λ, MCAR 30%, 7 traits, random coalescent trees. Missing-at-random, clade-structured missingness, OU evolution and real trees are not run here.
        • Discrete traits are scored at n = 100 and 300 only. They are thresholded Brownian liabilities, the model family BACE fits, while castor models one trait at a time with an Mk process and no covariates; no off-the-shelf phylogenetic frequentist method imputes discrete traits jointly with continuous ones.
        • The frequentist model shares one λ across its traits. prp carries extra non-phylogenetic noise in this design, so at λ near 1 its fitted variance is inflated (up to about 200-fold at n = 1000). The c1 and c2 estimands are not affected.

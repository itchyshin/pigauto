Chained BACE fixes most of the coverage gap; BACE's convergence check can't choose settings

Multiple imputation of phylogenetic traits, scored with Rubin's rules. 240 BACE fits on nibi compared BACE as shipped with a chained variant under six chain settings, on the same simulated datasets. Frequentist timing is measured too. Nothing beyond the pre-run has been launched.

Why BACE's convergence check can't choose settings

The selection rule asked for at least 80% of fits to pass BACE's own check. No setting came close, and more runs made it slightly worse. The installed code explains why: assess_convergence(method = "summary") tests the convergence phase, whose fills are deterministic (sample = FALSE), not the final draws. For each trait it tracks the mean imputed value per iteration and passes the trait if two of three criteria hold.

  • The percent-change criterion divides by the trait's mean. c1 and c2 are centred near zero in this simulation (mean level about 0.3), so small changes read as large percentages. prp, at level 0.53, passes 78% of the time; c1 and c2 pass 22%.
  • The autocorrelation criterion needs more iterations than BACE supplies. Pure white noise passes |acf| < 0.3 only of the time at runs 5, 10 and 15. At runs 5 the criterion fails most stationary series.
  • ESS says the chains mix. Median ESS across fits is 750 to 1,060 at n = 100 and reaches the 1,600-sample maximum at n = 300.

All twelve settings

20 fits per row. Wall times are medians on one nibi core; the chain adds 20 BACE sweeps to each fit.

Cost of the campaign

The frequentist arms are cheap: a fast exact PGLS (one eigendecomposition per tree, equal to nlme::gls to 1e-5) cut scoring at n = 1000 from about a minute per fit to milliseconds. BACE at n = 1000 dominates, and its cost there is extrapolated from v1 rather than measured with the chain.

Decisions for you

1

Drop BACE's convergence check from the selection rule

It tests the deterministic convergence phase, is scale-dependent, and did not respond to chain length. Select on ESS and on coverage stability instead.

Recommended: yes. Then the rule picks the cheapest setting, runs 5 and nitt 50,000, at both n.
2

Campaign arms

freq A (bootstrap then draw), freq B (one fit), BACE as shipped, chained BACE. BACE + residual shares the BACE fit, so it costs only scoring.

Recommended: all five, with BACE + residual reported as a negative control.
3

Replicates and budget

100 BACE replicates give a coverage SE of about 0.022; 200 give about 0.015. The n = 1000 BACE cost is an extrapolation, so run 6 fits there first (about 7 h wall on nibi).

Recommended: option B, about 7,400 core-hours on nibi arrays, after the n = 1000 timing check.
4

What to tell Dan, and when

Two findings concern BACE itself: its final runs share one starting dataset, which costs coverage, and its convergence check does not track mixing. Both are measured here, but only at n = 100 and 300.

Recommended: share after the campaign confirms the size of the effect, with the chained arm as a ready fix.

What this does not cover

  • Frequentist coverage. The frequentist runs were a timing check (2 seeds per cell); their coverage comes from the campaign.
  • n = 1000, λ = 1 and missing-at-random data. The pre-run used λ 0.3 and 0.7, n 100 and 300, MCAR only.
  • Independence of the 120 fits per n. They share 20 datasets; every SE on this page is clustered by dataset.
  • Discrete traits. Rubin scoring here covers c1, c2 and prp (logit scale); bin, ord and cat3 are imputed but not scored.
